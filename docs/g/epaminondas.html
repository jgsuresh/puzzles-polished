Epaminondas

At the start of your turn, have more stones on the enemy back row than they have on yours.

Each side starts with two full rows of stones. A phalanx is a straight, unbroken line of your stones (across, down or diagonally); a single stone is a phalanx of one.

Moving. A phalanx moves forwards or backwards along its own line, never sideways, up to as many squares as it has stones. You may also move just part of a phalanx (a shorter unbroken run), and then the limit is the number of stones you move. A single stone can step one square in any of the eight directions. Stones cannot jump over anything.

Capturing. If a moving phalanx runs into an enemy phalanx that is smaller than itself, along the direction of travel, it captures that whole enemy line and lands on its first square. Larger or equal lines block you.

Winning. The tinted rows are the goals. If, when your turn begins, you have more stones on your opponent's back row than they have on yours, you win.

How to play it here: tap a stone to select it, or tap two stones to select the run between them. The dots show where it can land (rings mean a capture), tap one to move. The 10 × 8 board is a smaller variant we added for phones.

Passing the phone? Tap a square to preview your move, then press Confirm (or tap the same square again) and hand the device over.
